## One Offer, One Audience, One Action

**Offer:** The 20-Hour Owner, a 10-week live founder operating-system cohort.

**Audience:** Established founders with healthy or growing revenue, an existing team, and at least 20 hours per week of coordination, approvals, follow-ups, or decisions still flowing through them.

**Action:** Submit an application for one of 10 seats.

**Core promise:** Install the systems, captured context, and delegation rhythm required to reclaim up to 20 hours per week without slowing the business.

## Grand Slam Offer Brief

| Variable | Decision |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome | A business that continues operating and growing without the founder carrying every decision. |
| Perceived likelihood | Kasim's documented operating history, a live implementation cohort, a second-brain build, and a small group of 10 founders. |

| Effort and sacrifice | Two live hours per week, using the founder's actual company as the implementation environment. |

## Offer Stack

1. **20 live group hours with Kasim** — 10 weekly sessions; cohort-adjusted reference value: $20,000.
2. **Done-with-you AI Second Brain** — context system for decisions, communication, and delegation; reference value: $3,000.
3. **12 months of founder community** — continuing peer access; reference value: $2,500.
4. **Delegation OS toolkit** — bottleneck audit, ownership map, escalation matrix, scorecard, and absence test; included.

## Inverse Interview: 20 Questions and Answers

### 1. What exact problem is the program solving?

The founder is the operating bottleneck. Approvals, follow-ups, exceptions, and institutional knowledge still route through one person.

### 2. Who has this problem intensely enough to pay for help?

Established founders with real teams and healthy or growing revenue whose calendars are still dominated by lower-leverage execution.

### 3. Who is not a fit?

Pre-revenue founders, solo operators without a team, passive course buyers, and anyone looking for a generic productivity program.

### 4. What does the buyer want instead?

More control over their time, fewer dropped balls, stronger ownership across the team, and confidence that the business can function without constant intervention.

### 5. What is the measurable promise?

Reclaim up to 20 hours per week by the end of a 10-week implementation cohort.

### 6. Why should the buyer believe the mechanism?

The mechanism combines a bottleneck audit, captured context, explicit decision rights, outcome-based delegation, escalation rules, and an absence test. It changes the operating system rather than asking the founder to work faster.

### 7. Why Kasim?

His public founder story centers on building and exiting an 8-figure agency through exceptional people and ownership rather than personally performing the technical work.

### 8. What is the unique mechanism?

The 20-Hour Operating System: map, capture, transfer, and remove. It combines human ownership with an AI-ready context layer.

### 9. What happens in the first two weeks?

The founder maps their calendar, recurring decisions, dropped balls, and work that should no longer reach them.

### 10. What makes the outcome faster?

The work is live, small-cohort, and applied to the founder's existing company. The implementation starts in week one rather than after a curriculum is completed.

### 11. What reduces effort for the buyer?

Templates, guided audits, a second-brain structure, clear weekly milestones, and a two-hour live cadence.

### 12. What objections will the page need to handle?

“I do not have time,” “my business is too complex,” “my team is not ready,” “I have tried assistants before,” and “delegation lowers quality.”

### 13. How does the program answer the assistant objection?

It is not an assistant-placement promise. It teaches the founder to transfer outcomes, context, and decision space so a capable operator can succeed.

### 14. What proof can be used safely?

Kasim's public founder history and Pareto Talent's publicly reported founder-served and retention metrics. No invented testimonials or private client results are used.

### 15. What proof is still missing?

Approved time-back testimonials from previous founders, audited cohort outcomes, and founder approval for all public metrics.

### 16. Why is the cohort limited to 10 seats?

The offer requires direct work on each founder's operating system. A small cohort protects implementation depth and creates real scarcity.

### 17. What is included in the guarantee?

In the concept, a participant can use the first 14 days to test the fit and receive a full refund if the program is not the right vehicle.

### 18. What single action should every section support?

Submitting an application. Secondary links only move the visitor to supporting sections on the same page.

### 19. What tone should the page use?

Direct, founder-level, mechanism-first, practical, and free of generic motivation. It should contrast activity with leverage and tasks with ownership.

### 20. What must be approved before launch?

The program name, promise, cohort dates, price, value stack, guarantee terms, public metrics, founder bio language, and application destination.
